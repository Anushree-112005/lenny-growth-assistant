## The Most Underrated Growth Lever: Pricing Strategy

In conversations with over 100 high-growth startup founders, pricing is almost universally recognized as the single highest-leverage growth lever, yet the one startups spend the least time experimenting with. 

Monetization touches everything: unit economics, positioning, retention, and CAC payback period. Most founders set their price once when launching, based on a gut check or copying a legacy incumbent, and don't touch it for three years.

## The Three Core Pricing Models in B2B SaaS

### 1. Cost-Plus Pricing (Avoid this)
Taking your infrastructure and labor costs and adding a 30% margin. This completely ignores the economic value delivered to the buyer and leaves massive surplus on the table.

### 2. Competitor-Based Pricing
Pricing at 80% or 100% of the dominant market leader. While useful as a sanity check, it anchors your product as a commodity replacement rather than a differentiated solution.

### 3. Value-Based Pricing (The Gold Standard)
Pricing directly proportional to the perceived economic value or ROI the customer derives from your software. If your tool saves a support department \$100,000 annually in headcount, charging \$15,000 is an immediate 6.6x ROI for the buyer.

## Choosing the Right Value Metric

The value metric is the primary unit of measurement by which you charge (e.g., per user/seat, per gigabyte stored, per active contact, per transaction processed).

An ideal value metric satisfies three criteria:
1. Aligns with customer value: As the customer extracts more business success from your product, the price naturally expands without friction.
2. Easy to predict and understand: Buyers hate unexpected bill shocks at month-end.
3. Hard to game: If charging by seats, teams will share generic logins (info@company.com). If charging by usage (e.g., messages sent or events logged), the customer feels fair parity.

HubSpot famously shifted from charging per user seat to charging by number of marketing contacts in their CRM database. That single packaging pivot drove hundreds of millions of dollars in automatic annual expansion revenue.
