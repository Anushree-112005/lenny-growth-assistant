## The Core Foundations of Product-Led Growth (PLG) in B2B

Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to the podcast. Today I'm speaking with Elena Verna, growth advisor to Miro, Amplitude, Dropbox, and SurveyMonkey. Elena, let's start with a definition: what does product-led growth really mean for a modern B2B SaaS company?

Elena Verna: PLG is not just a free trial button or a freemium pricing tier. PLG is a go-to-market model where the product itself drives acquisition, retention, and monetization. Instead of hiring an army of SDRs and outbound sales reps to convince people to buy software they've never touched, you let the end-user experience the value first. 

The most common misconception is that PLG replaces sales. In reality, the most successful enterprise companies run a hybrid model: Product-Led Sales (PLS). The product handles the bottom-up adoption, creating thousands of active end-users inside organizations. Then, and only then, your sales team steps in with enterprise contracts, security compliance, centralized billing, and executive rollouts.

## Linear Funnels vs. Compounding Growth Loops

Lenny Rachitsky: You often criticize traditional marketing funnels. Why are funnels flawed, and how should founders think about growth loops instead?

Elena Verna: Funnels are fundamentally linear and transactional. You pour \$1 into top-of-funnel paid acquisition, and if your conversion rate is 3%, you get a customer, but the loop ends there. To get another customer, you have to pour another dollar in. That does not scale sustainably.

Growth loops, on the other hand, are closed compounding systems. In a growth loop:
1. An input generates an action by a user.
2. That action produces an output.
3. The output is reinvested directly into generating more inputs or new users.

Take Miro or Figma as classic collaborative loops: User A signs up to create a digital whiteboard or wireframe. To make it useful, User A invites Users B, C, and D to collaborate. Users B, C, and D experience the core product value immediately without seeing an ad. Eventually, User C leaves that meeting, creates their own board for a different project, and invites Users E and F. That is an organic viral product loop.

Another loop is content-driven SEO: A user generates a public template or question on your platform. Search engines index that page. A new visitor discovers the page, gets value, and signs up.

## The Three Pillars of PLG Monetization

Lenny Rachitsky: When should a B2B product trigger the paywall? How do you prevent giving away too much value for free?

Elena Verna: There are three primary monetization vectors in PLG:
1. Feature Gates: Locking sophisticated features needed only by mature teams (like SSO, audit logs, advanced permissions, and dedicated support).
2. Usage Limits: Giving users unlimited time to experience core value, but capping volume—such as number of active projects, API calls, or stored contacts.
3. Seat-Based Collaboration Gates: Allowing single-player mode for free, but requiring a team subscription once more than 3 collaborators join a workspace.

The cardinal rule of PLG is: Never gate the "Aha!" moment. If users cannot reach value before seeing a credit card form, they will bounce to an open alternative. Let them fall in love with the product, build a habit, and then charge for scale and governance.
